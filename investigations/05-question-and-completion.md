# Investigation 05 — The Question and the Completion

**24 November 2026 · Curator's insight and proposed research instrument · Cross-cell investigation**

## The originating recognition

The curator asked:

> What is a question if not a request for completion....and if this is the case and that we consider 'any sentence' to effectively be the answer to a specific question then t might be possible to use the question to identify exactly what was open

This is a pivotal proposal for Wonder. Instead of approaching completed intelligibility only from its achieved side, investigate the demand to which the achievement answers. A question can articulate not merely missing information but what would count as enough for a particular undertaking.

**The question discloses what is open. The answer discloses what has been achieved. Their correspondence discloses whether the undertaking has been fulfilled.**

## The two directions of recognition

The existing method asks what changed, what made the change sufficient, and how the achievement remains available. The proposed complementary instrument works in both directions:

- From question to answer: identify the unresolvedness, anticipate its sufficient completion, and examine whether the actual answer accomplishes it.
- From answer to question: recover the most precise implicit demand to which the sentence would be sufficient, without importing an unsupported history or intended classification.
- Compare the two: does the recovered demand correspond to the original undertaking, including its scale, conditions and requested outcome?

The question is not an additional primitive dimension. It may disclose the demand whose completion is already examined by the three canonical questions. The Operative Centre remains the governing determination that constitutes the actual achievement, not the interrogative wording itself.

## Six illustrative pairs

| Question | Answer | Candidate transformation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which one is hers? | The one with the blue ribbon. | Differentiation |
| How can we get inside? | There's an entrance at the back. | Opening |
| What connects these events? | They both arose from the same decision. | Relating |
| How can we put it right? | Adjust the hinge until the door fits. | Repair (requested; actual repair not yet established) |
| How do these parts hold together? | Each supports the others. | Integration (the described structure; explanatory accomplishment requires separate examination) |
| Which proposal have we accepted? | We've chosen the second one. | Settlement |

These are illustrative, not independently resolved coordinates. Notice especially that an instruction can answer a question about repair without accomplishing the physical repair. The achievement of the utterance and the achievement it describes or enables must remain distinct.

## Three neighbouring forms

- **01 — Focal mark.** What is that bright object in the sky? — That's Venus. The demand is identification at a decisive particular.
- **02 — Binary bearing.** What makes her voice stand out among the others? — Hers is the only voice that remains steady. The demand is distinction constituted through contrast.
- **03 — Boundary act.** At what point must we stop admitting people? — Once the last seat is taken, nobody else may enter. The demand concerns an operative discriminator.

The question may anticipate more than the transformation: it may disclose what constitutes sufficiency and what must remain available. Whether it does so uniquely across all 54 conjunctions is a research question, not a result established by these known-answer examples.

## The qualification that protects the discovery

A question need not have been literally spoken. Assertions, promises, commands and expressions of feeling may answer implicit demands in a broader sense. Some utterances initiate a demand rather than discharge one; some texts remain incomplete. A sentence may answer different questions in different contexts, and a single question may admit structurally different sufficient answers. Do not invent a unique historical question merely because a plausible question can be constructed.

For example, the same utterance, "The door is shut," may answer a question about its position but fail to answer why the room is still wet. Likewise, the instruction to adjust a hinge does not prove that the hinge has been adjusted. An adequate method must distinguish the content asserted, the linguistic achievement, the undertaking and its satisfaction in the world.

This extends Investigation 04: the same scene can resolve different demands. It also distinguishes local completion from completion of the entire exchange. A recovered question can be narrower than the original one, and a useful partial answer need not be falsely promoted to completion.

## Application to artificial intelligence

Original user question: **Why does my computer keep shutting down?**

Possible AI response: "Computers can shut down because of overheating, power supply problems or software faults."

Question recovered from that response: **What are some possible causes of computers shutting down?**

The difference identifies a concrete insufficiency. The response opens a field of diagnostic possibilities but does not establish the explanation of this particular computer's behaviour. That opening may be useful and appropriate when evidence is missing, provided the AI acknowledges what remains open and seeks the necessary evidence.

An AI could use the following discipline:

1. Identify the actual undertaking before answering, including the conditions of adequacy.
2. Anticipate what kind of achievement would suffice, without mistaking the anticipated achievement for an accomplished one.
3. Produce the response or execute the required action.
4. Recover the question actually answered by the response, preferably through an independent assessor blind to the original question.
5. Compare the recovered and original demands; verify any factual or practical claims independently.
6. Record what was achieved, what remains open and what further undertaking is justified.

This is a proposed **question–answer correspondence test**, not a demonstrated improvement in AI reliability. Structural correspondence alone cannot establish factual truth or successful execution.

## A research programme using Wonder's 540 sentences

Wonder currently preserves ten constructed sentences for each of the 54 cells. Use these as a candidate corpus, not a verified gold standard.

- Freeze the existing sentences and intended addresses. Generate natural candidate questions, retaining alternative questions and ambiguous cases.
- Give a fresh assessor only each question and any necessary context; ask what kind of sufficient completion it demands, using the primitive definitions but not the intended cell.
- Give a separate assessor only the answer and appropriate context; recover the implicit question and governing completion. Use the canonical blind assay and decoder where applicable, preserving their information barriers.
- Compare question-predicted and answer-resolved coordinates with each other and with the intended address. Include strongest rivals, incomplete cases and uncertainty; do not force agreement.
- Introduce negative controls: correct but irrelevant answers, plausible partial answers, different demands on the same scene, false but intelligible assertions, and questions with more than one legitimate completion.
- Compare the method with ordinary relevance checking and a conventional task-completion checklist. Measure agreement, detection of unmet demands and false claims of completion, not merely cell accuracy.

No blind runs have yet been performed for this investigation. Keep the question and answer frozen before adjudication, and retain disagreements as evidence.

## New development — from the 54 answer forms to 54 question forms

**24 November 2026 · General-form investigation · A step towards knowing the 54 more closely**

The study has reached a more precise possibility. We already possess fifty-four canonical forms of **completed** intelligibility. Instead of beginning with the grammatical interrogatives *who, what, when, where, how* and *why*, we can begin with those established answer forms and ask what **characteristic demand for sufficiency** would call for each one.

This is not the invention of another set of fifty-four primitives. It is a proposed reversal of view within the same grammar:

1. Begin with the existing canonical cell and its unified completed achievement.
2. Articulate, without choosing a subject matter, what makes that achievement sufficient.
3. Ask what remains unresolved **until just such a sufficiency is available**.
4. Formulate a general question that makes that particular demand recognisable.
5. Return from question to completion and inspect whether the demand really anticipates the intended achievement, or whether neighbouring completions could satisfy it equally well.

A characteristic question is **not necessarily a unique question**, a historically spoken question, or an algorithmically reversible encoding. A single question can admit several structurally different sufficient answers. Similarly, a completed utterance can answer more than one governing requirement in different contexts. The research question is whether every cell can sustain a useful, discriminating *general interrogative counterpart*.

### An initial six-family vocabulary of demands

| Completed transformation | Provisional characteristic question |
| --- | --- |
| Differentiation | Which is which? |
| Opening | What is possible? |
| Relating | What bears on what? |
| Repair | What would put it right? |
| Integration | How do these belong together? |
| Settlement | What would be enough? |

These are entrances to the six families, **not** definitions of them. The interrogative word alone does not determine the Transformation Pattern. For example, *How can I get inside?*, *How are they related?*, *How can I fix it?*, *How do the parts work together?*, *How was it settled?*, and *How can I distinguish them?* all begin with *how* yet articulate different demands. The whole question, rather than a word extracted from it, gives the unresolvedness its shape.

### Three neighbouring cells, viewed from both sides

| Cell | General completion | Candidate general question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **01 — Focal mark** | A particular is determinately distinguished at one constitutive locus; its determination stands. | What is this particular thing? |
| **02 — Binary bearing** | A particular is distinguished through a constitutive contrast on which its determination bears. | Which is this, in distinction from that? |
| **03 — Boundary act** | A distinction is completed at a decisive locus as an operative discrimination. | At what boundary does this difference become decisive for what may proceed? |

The questions are deliberately about **what would be enough**, not about a preferred topic or a particular story. They are preliminary: an apparently suitable question may also be answered by another cell. The test of quality is whether it exposes the cell's distinctive sufficient achievement rather than merely repeating its name.

The same reversal can be attempted beyond Differentiation:

| Cell | General completion | Candidate general question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **37 — Focal whole** | Separate contributions belong to one whole through a constitutive centre. | What makes these contributions one whole around this centre? |
| **40 — Role array** | A whole is constituted across differentiated positions or roles. | How do these distinct positions together constitute the whole? |
| **45 — Convergent whole** | Separate undertakings become one operative undertaking when their independent claims to govern no longer determine action. | What has made these formerly separate undertakings capable of acting as one, beyond their competing claims? |

For Cell 45, merely asking what *would allow* joint action could elicit a proposal without an achieved integration. The question form must be considered in relation to whether it demands an accomplished whole, an explanation of such a whole, or a merely possible means to produce one.

### Three analytically different objects

**The question as a completed utterance:** asking itself establishes a determinate inquiry. Its intelligibility can be complete while the answer remains open.

**The completion demanded by the question:** the structural sufficiency that would answer the inquiry appropriately.

**The answer as a completed utterance:** what the actual words achieve, which may be sufficient, partial, or differently organised from what the question anticipated.

These must not be collapsed into a single cell. Recovering a question can expose the governing requirement without independently revealing the internal sufficiency organisation of every possible answer. The distinction makes the proposed reversal philosophically useful rather than merely a mechanical transformation.

### The Wonder study programme

The next research instrument is a **provisional 54-row question-form companion**, one row for each existing cell, preserving the canonical form on one side and an independently intelligible characteristic question on the other. This is a general-form study first, not the automatic addition of 540 questions to the sentence gallery.

For each candidate, test a neighbouring rival and ask whether the question actually demands the relevant sufficiency. Only then use Wonder's varied ordinary sentences to explore how the same general demand appears in different worlds. Record ambiguous, shared, and non-unique question forms rather than force one-to-one correspondence.

The result sought is greater familiarity with the internal character of all fifty-four forms: not only **how each completed achievement holds together**, but **what particular unresolvedness could call for it**.

**Working proposition:** The canonical completion form discloses what can become sufficient. Its characteristic question form discloses the demand for that sufficiency. Their relation is an object of study, not yet a proven equivalence.

## Open questions

1. Can the form of a demand constrain all three dimensions of its anticipated completion, or only some of them?
2. When does recovering a question genuinely disclose the unresolvedness rather than retrospectively fitting an answer?
3. How should we distinguish a question that requests an achievement from one that asks for a description of an achievement?
4. Can the correspondence test detect an AI's failure to complete a task more reliably than existing methods?
5. Does the grammar of completed intelligibility have a corresponding grammar of intelligible demand, and what would establish that claim?
