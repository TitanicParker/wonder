# Wonder · Investigation 02
## When does a rack become a whole?
*Source-grounded comparison of Cells 39, 41 and 42 · 22 November 2026*

**Status:** Provisional scholarly investigation, not a change to the canonical table. This note distinguishes source statements from curator inference and leaves unresolved cases unresolved.

### The encounter

In *Maya's Bike*, Jo and Sam build a rack from rails, hooks, brackets and fixings. A rack may look complete yet drop under the bicycle's weight. After a fixing is relocated into a stud and the rack is tested again, it holds. The narrative expressly uses that episode to introduce **Integration × Distributed × Efficacy**: the parts have become a working arrangement whose capacity is distributed through the organized set. It also insists that the episode itself is a *carrier*, not the completed intelligibility.

That is a useful place to test a temptation: to call anything that works as a whole **Joint capacity** (Cell 39). But Cell 39 is **Integration × Focal × Efficacy**, not Distributed. The word *capacity* alone cannot decide the topology.

### Three canonical neighbours

| Cell | Historical X–Y traversal | Coordinate | What the source makes salient |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **39 · Joint capacity** | “X and Y interlock to form system Z.” | Integration × Focal × Efficacy | A joint capacity achieved through a privileged coordinating whole or centre |
| **41 · Mutual-support structure** | “X and Y support one another’s constraints.” | Integration × Distributed × Bearing | A whole whose continuing identity is constituted by interdependent support |
| **42 · Role activation** | “X fills its role within system Y.” | Integration × Distributed × Efficacy | An organized plurality whose roles now operate together |

These expressions are preserved as *historical traversals*, not translations that fully determine the coordinates. Their X and Y are not necessarily fixed objects, and the grammatical appearance of “system Z” does not by itself establish Focal topology.

### The governing question changes

**39 — Joint capacity.** Suppose the question is whether the contributions have become *one coordinated agency* able to accomplish what neither could alone. The focal whole, rather than each supporting relation, is decisive; the residue is the whole's operative ability. This is a candidate reading of the coordinate, **not** a claim that every assembled rack exemplifies Cell 39.

**41 — Mutual-support structure.** Suppose the question is whether the rack's components *hold one another in a coherent arrangement*. The support is not an incidental means of discovering the answer; it constitutes what remains. Removing a relevant bearing undoes the achieved structure. The achievement is Distributed and persists as Bearing, even if the rack could also be used.

**42 — Role activation.** Suppose the question is whether hooks, rail, brace and fixings *now perform their coordinated work under load*. No single fixing supplies the capacity; the organized set does. What remains is effective operation. This matches the explicit rack analysis in *Maya's Bike*.

The same rack can carry each question without the questions being equivalent. Conversely, a different physical system could exhibit the same form. These are hypotheses about how to interrogate the scene, not three labels that must be assigned to it.

### A counterfactual test, not a word-matching test

Ask what must fail for the *particular achievement* to be lost.

- If the decisive loss is the **single coordinating whole** through which joint agency was achieved, consider 39.
- If the decisive loss is a **constitutive relation of mutual support**, consider 41.
- If the decisive loss is **distributed role performance**, consider 42.

These are diagnostic prompts, not sufficient classification rules. A real rack can lose one fixing and thereby lose its structure *and* its operation. In that case the investigator must recover the live question before treating the observed failure as evidence for one coordinate.

### What Persistence changes in this comparison

The *Constraint Grammar* monograph separates **coordinate-generated form** from **canonical crystallization**. It also separates **historical formation** (how an achievement arose) from **coordinate grammar** (what completed form it has). The intellectual reconstruction warns that canonical names may be more specific than their coordinates strictly force. Thus the word *Joint* or *Mutual* cannot be treated as an independent primitive, nor may the X–Y wording silently replace the triple.

This yields a stricter method for Wonder:

1. Recover the unresolved unit **before** choosing a cell.
2. Ask what transformation occurred, what made it sufficient, and what persists.
3. Derive a provisional coordinate **without** consulting the canonical name.
4. Compare the resulting form with the historical X–Y traversal and canonical crystallization, recording both resonance and discrepancy.
5. Preserve plausible rival readings until the scene supplies discriminating evidence.

### A possible family — deliberately not asserted

All three cells concern integration, but this is already given by the first axis and is not a newly discovered family. Their historical expressions all use relational grammar (interlock; support one another; fill a role within), yet that lexical resemblance is insufficient to prove a second, overlapping X–Y family. A future investigation must ask whether the *kind of relation expressed* recurs across other Transformation Patterns without collapsing their distinct achievements.

**Open questions:** Can a scene isolate Focal Efficacy from Distributed Efficacy without changing the object? When is support the achieved residue rather than a means toward operation? Does the X–Y grammar mark these distinctions reliably, or does it require context that the expressions themselves cannot supply?
